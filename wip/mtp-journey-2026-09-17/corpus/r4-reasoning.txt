# A deduction puzzle with an uncertain witness

Read the whole problem before you start. Reason step by step and state every inference explicitly.
Give the final conclusions only at the very end.

Five sealed boxes, labelled A through E, each contain exactly one coloured stone. The possible colours
are red, blue, green, yellow and white, and every colour is used at least once. Four witnesses each
make exactly one statement, and exactly two of the four statements are true. The witnesses are known to
be unreliable in the sense that a false statement is false, but a true statement may be misleadingly
phrased.

The statements:

- Witness 1: "Box A is red if and only if box C is not green."
- Witness 2: "Exactly one of box B and box D is yellow."
- Witness 3: "Box E is white unless box B is red, in which case box E is blue."
- Witness 4: "The boxes A, B and C contain no colour in common with the boxes C, D and E respectively."

Determine every assignment of colours that is consistent with all four statements and with exactly two
of them being true. For each consistent assignment, list which witnesses are truthful.

Then answer:

- How many consistent assignments are there, and how many distinct colour multisets of the five stones
  do they realise?
- Is the colour of box D determined in every consistent assignment? Prove or give a counterexample.
- If the witnesses are instead known to be exactly one truthful and three false, does any consistent
  assignment survive? Explain fully.
- Finally, if a sixth box F is added containing the one colour not yet placed, restate witness 2 as a
  statement about the whole collection and determine whether the count of consistent assignments rises
  or falls. Justify your answer with an explicit count.
