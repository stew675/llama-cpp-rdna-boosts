**1. The sub-complement budget alone does NOT enter the band.**  At the delivery's default arena the item's own

an **explicitly capped** `MOE_EXPERT_CACHE_MIB` -- and the cap's requirement scales with the widest batch served
(8 x 16 = 128 slots/table at the band top), so `2560` fixes `-npl 8` and leaves `-npl 16` at 66.2 against 149.0

470-483 slots/table, `decline_all=0`, 106.0 tg -- nowhere near this bound; the `device remap: 27 651 of 27 651

## 261112.009 (r273, RELEASE v17-a691b2345-r273) -- the page-cache amplification, the pool exchange's missing guard, and `-lme N` finally meaning N

**Release:** `v17-a691b2345-r273` -- 17 blocks (block 00 + blocks 01-16), base `a691b2345`, canonical re-cut tip
**`6245ba844`**, tree **`2f4f24f46608492fa91c6eaa61dab49887eeb43e`**.  Blocks 09 and 16 amended.  Three new environment
variables (`MOE_HOST_POOL_BULK_DIO`, `MOE_HOST_POOL_PREWARM_RESERVE_MIB`, `LLAMA_LAZY_MLOCK_RESERVE_MIB`); `-lme`'s
refusal and `MOE_HOST_POOL_RAM_PCT`'s reach both re-scoped.  **No numerics move: every arm byte-identical**
(`a99ccbd97ecb` on the gate's config, `8c39335bbceb` on the split pair), prefill-logit KLD **0.000000 / 100.000 %**.

### What the report actually was

The maintainer reported that `--load-mode buffered` cut decode to half the `--load-mode dio` rate while the disk ran
at 1-1.2 GB/s, on Flash-Next Q4_K_XL (`-ncmoe 36 -lme 70`, 2 x R9700, `-sm tensor`), and that the pool claimed to be a
mirror while behaving as if it barely existed.  Two independent defects turned out to be stacked, plus three smaller
ones the investigation exposed.

1. **The pool was being emptied by its own exchange.**  On a `-sm tensor` split each device owns a SLICE of every
   expert, so the arena->pool demote (one contiguous D2H of a whole expert) can never be expressed --
   **766 758 geometry declines out of 766 758 attempts**.  `pool_sink_active()` asked only "is the sink on and the
   pool a sub-mirror?", so the exchange was "engaged": it reserved ~4 % of every table's slots for a demote that
   could never land AND retired the pool's copy of every expert it promoted to VRAM, on the assumption that a
   just-evicted expert would take the vacancy.  Nothing ever did.  Census: **792 321 retirements against 52 704
   slots, 701 706 refills, h=0.3473, 0 evictions** -- ~695 GiB of host reads across one 317 s generation, which is
   the 1-1.2 GB/s of disk.  `MOE_HOST_POOL_SINK=0` was the workaround (h=0.9221, **24.17 -> 59.24 t/s**).
   The guard the code declared for this (`g_sink_need_slot`, "makes the retirement demand-driven") was written three
   times and never read.  Fixed structurally: `host_pool_t::demote_ok`, decided once from the table geometry at pool
   build, now gates the whole exchange (no reserve, no demote, no retirement, no churn).
2. **The placement read doubled the footprint in the page cache.**  The pinned pool is not the read buffer: a
   buffered `pread` materialises the file's pages in the page cache and then copies them into the pool.  Measured
   (35B-A3B Q8_0, 32 640 MiB mirror, cold cache): page-cache high-water **+33.7 GiB buffered against +3.9 GiB with
   O_DIRECT**, for 4.9 s against 6.8 s of fill.  On the maintainer's box that is ~150 GiB of demand on 91.4 GiB, and
   it cannot be reclaimed afterwards: the expert tensors stay MAPPED (they are the pool-backed master), and
   `posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)` skips mapped pages.  r215 built this path for O_DIRECT and r262's transport mirror
   silently made it follow `--load-mode`.  The placement read is now **O_DIRECT whatever `--load-mode` says**
   (`MOE_HOST_POOL_BULK_DIO=0` = the A/B); `--load-mode` remains the demand-read transport.
3. **The budget's provenance decides the ceiling, and the resolver now runs first.**  An explicit absolute budget
   (`-lme N`, `MOE_HOST_POOL_MIB`) is honoured in full; a derived one (`-lme auto`'s resolution, FRAC/COV) keeps
   `MOE_HOST_POOL_RAM_PCT`.  The ceiling had been shaving `-lme 70` to 95.3 % silently, and the mirror/near-mirror
   classification ran BEFORE the clamp -- which is how the census said "108 mirror tables, exchange INERT" about a
   pool that was 95.3 % and engaged.  A RESOLVED-layout WARN now reports coverage, shape, exchange state and budget
   class once, after the number is final.
4. **`-lme 75` was refused with a message claiming 80 was allowed.**  The test compared against 90 % of MemAvailable
   while the message printed the allowance rounded to whole GiB.  Now: refused only at **or above 100 % of
   MemAvailable**, with exact units, and 85-100 % is a WARNING (verified: `-lme 85` refused at 105 %, `-lme 75`
   accepted at 92.8 %).
5. **Two irreversible pins got headroom gates.**  The pool prewarm (`MOE_HOST_POOL_PREWARM_RESERVE_MIB`, default
   1024) defers a pool it cannot place -- pinned memory is never given back, and `h` still converges to 1.0000 on a
   run whose whole prewarm was deferred.  `--lazy-mode mlock`/`mlockr` (`LLAMA_LAZY_MLOCK_RESERVE_MIB`, default
   1024) decline the populate of a table larger than what is left and leave it on the on-demand path, which is
   exactly `--lazy-mode auto`: measured on Flash-Next, `mlock DECLINED: the marked tables are 27466.0 MiB and only
   82376.9 MiB is available`.

### The gate was blind to the defect, and that is now closed

`gate-host-pool.sh` ran **one** device, where `expert_bytes == host_bytes` and the demote always succeeds, so the
annihilator in (1) was unreachable: the gate was green **41/41** through the entire defect.  Two arms added --
`tsplit` and `tsplitnosink` (2 devices, `-sm tensor`, same pool, `MOE_HOST_POOL_SINK=1` vs `0`): `tsplit` must build
a real pool, report the sink census, and show the exchange **INERT** (`0 demoted, 0 geometry declines, 0
retirements`), and the two arms must generate byte-identical text.  The single-GPU `sink` arm still proves the demote
is exercised where it *can* be, so the pair cannot pass by the sink silently going global-off.

### Also landed: released uploads are dropped from the page cache

`LLAMA_LOAD_RELEASE_MMAP` unmaps a device-bound tensor's range after its H2D, and now also
`posix_fadvise(POSIX_FADV_DONTNEED)`s it -- which is only possible after the unmap, and is the cheap half of "do not
flush the system page cache for weights that are already placed".  Measured on a 4.3 GiB model: peak `Cached` during
load **5.15 -> 1.99 GiB**.  This also explains the maintainer's "decode got significantly faster": the load was
reclaiming exactly the pages the decode band uses (the 26.8 GiB lazy PLE, read every token).

### The report (contributor PR #304, `briansp2020`, written by Claude on their behalf)

For `n == 2` the bf16 large-tensor arm ran a reduce-scatter (each rank ships half its partial, the owner adds) then an
all-gather of the reduced halves plus a `to_fp32`.  Their +43 lines send **each rank's whole bf16 partial to the peer
once** and form every element as `fp32(bf16(fp32(own) + fp32(peer)))` in one kernel.  Claims: same bits, same link
bytes, one cross-device dependency instead of two, no separate add / convert passes; `GGML_CUDA_AR_CE_SINGLE=0`
restores the two-phase form; `n == 2` only.  Their measurements were on 2x R9700 (x8 each) with 2x Flash-Next:
+2.2-5.5 % prefill on their host-pool arm, +0.3-1.0 % all-VRAM, decode unchanged, replies byte-identical.

### Assessment

* **The bit-identity claim is right.**  Both forms compute the same expression from the same two staged bf16 partials
  (the two-phase owner's `ggml_cuda_ce_add_bf16` + all-gather + `to_fp32`; the new kernel's identical
  `__float2bfloat16` cast), and fp32 addition is commutative, so every element and every rank agree.  Verified the
  strong way rather than by text: `llama-perplexity --kl-divergence-base` writes the per-position log-probs of the
  whole vocabulary, **5 065 891 540 B** for 40 x 512, and the two arms' files are **`cmp`-identical**.  Same-seed
  greedy text is `10a82b5559a9` in all four arms (2 devices either form, 3 devices, the previous binary).
* **The gate that reaches it is not the documented one.**  The delivery's all-reduce arm is gated at
  `--sm tensor --device 0,1,2`, and 3 ranks never enter this branch.  The reaching arm is `--device 0,2` (the x8
  pair): mean KLD **-0.000000 / 100.000 %** with the base recorded from the two-phase form.
* **What it buys here: +1.0-1.4 % prefill, decode flat.**  27B Q8_0, `-sm tensor` 0,2, `GGML_CUDA_ALLREDUCE=ce`,
  `llama-bench -p 512,2048,4096 -n 64 -r 3`, two interleaved rounds: pp512 2298.6/2289.8 -> **2317.4/2315.4**, pp2048
  2257.9/2255.1 -> **2285.4/2284.6**, pp4096 2230.9/2229.8 -> **2254.8/2255.6**, tg64 flat (33.4-33.9 both).  Smaller
  than the submitter's host-pool figure and larger than their all-VRAM one, which is the expected shape for a change
  that removes one kernel pass: it is a bigger fraction of a prefill that is not already waiting on host reads.
* **Their biggest number is not resolvable on this box, and the attempt found a delivery bug instead.**  A 2-device
  host-expert arm (`Flash-Next IQ3_S`, `-ncmoe 47 -lme 47`, 5 246-token prompt, warm runs) has a 4-6 % spread within

* **Two things the contributed cut lacked, both required by policy.**  The arm had **no census surface at all** -- a

## 261112.007 (r271, release) -- the seed-policy record names the arm it measured: `auto` is INERT on a covering pool, the mirror loss is `lazy`'s alone

**`b5395bf8df59d9f938da8c2acafaf4ec2bd7bdbe`**, tree **`5ff217df618e673e96e5014074911c8b24595f98`**.  A correction to r270's

### What was wrong

r270's block-16 comment and its table summarised the mirror-pool arm as "`auto`/`lazy` cost TTFT +5.1 s ... decode
49.2 -> 40.0 t/s".  Only `lazy` was measured there.  `auto` is a different arm by construction -- it PLACES a tier
that covers its tensor and defers only a subset, so on a mirror pool it defers the *arena* seed and keeps the pool
prewarm.  Measured now (`soar`, `-lme auto` = the whole 40 012 MiB set, 57-token prompt, 500-token greedy decode,
cold page cache, text `6e6d241acc25` in all three arms):

| arm | TTFT | wall | prompt eval | decode | device remap | reads |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| `eager` | 13.58 s | 22.21 s | 0.68 s | 49.17 t/s | served | 46 GiB |
| **`auto`** | **13.30 s** | **22.19 s** | **0.66 s** | **49.44 t/s** | **served** | 45 GiB |
| `lazy` | 18.71 s | 31.14 s | 8.01 s | 40.01 t/s | stands down | 51 GiB |

So `auto` is **inert** on a covering pool: the covering tier is placed (complete, so the remap's cold source holds),
and the deferred arena seed is re-earned by the prefill within noise.  The -18.6 % decode belongs to `lazy` alone,
i.e. to deferring a tier that must be complete -- which is exactly the rule `GREEDY-PURITY.md` §87 states.

`INVARIANTS.md` r270 (tagged `(r271)`), `ENVIRONMENT.md` §20, `patches/README.md` block 16, `GREEDY-PURITY.md` §87.

RANK evictions)".  The item also required ONE policy for both tiers, because the discrete host pool's prewarm and
57 s cold fill are "the same decision".

### The answer: measured on both tiers, and it is `eager`

The fill is not a fixed cost -- it is the **prefetch the demand path would otherwise pay inline**.  Deferring it
does not save the bytes: total storage reads are **45-46 GiB in every arm** on the discrete tier at the delivered
budgets, because a 500-token decode's byte range is the whole host set either way.  What changes is *where* the cost
lands, and on the discrete tier it lands badly:

* **the discrete pool tier has no in-place prefill band.**  `pf_in_place_locked` is `g_gtt_arena` only, so a
  prefill there stages the whole expert table into the op's `input_cpy` and prefetches the **complement** whether
  the pass routes it or not (a routing-aware staging needs the host ids readback the gather design avoids, r265).
  Deferring the seed moves that whole read into the prompt, one cold per-expert read at a time -- **prompt eval
  0.88 s -> 7.24 s** on a 57-token prompt, **7.63 s -> 17.18 s** on a 5 246-token one (694 -> 308 t/s), decode flat.
* **a covering pool with holes loses the sync-free decode remap.**  On the `-lme auto` mirror pool (the whole
  40 012 MiB set) the lazy arm served **0** remap ops against 70 779, and decode fell **49.17 -> 40.01 t/s
  (-18.6 %)** for byte-identical text (`devremap_prep_locked` refuses a cold map with a hole).
* **on an APU the deferred seed is a real window, and not a default.**  `halo`, 122B-A10B Q5_K_XL on a `-lme 70`
  store (225 of 256 slots/table): TTFT **47.77 -> 28.99 s (-39 %)** and a 32-token answer 50.3 -> 44.5 s (-12 %),
  but decode **11.73 -> 4.83 t/s** and **+70 GiB** of reads over a 250-token answer (236 vs 166 GiB).

So the tiers share the QUESTION and the knob, not the answer: `eager` ships as the default, `auto` (defer a tier
that holds a subset, place a tier that covers its tensor -- the APU policy ported verbatim) and `lazy` are the
measured arms, and the APU short-answer window is documented in `ENVIRONMENT.md` rather than defaulted.  A side
finding is recorded as an invariant: the AUTO pool budget is a function of `MemAvailable` *at the first pool build*,
so an A/B against it must pin the budget (`-lme N` + `MOE_HOST_POOL_RAM_PCT=0`) or the arms differ in tier *and*
policy (this cost the first cut of the measurement: 33.6 GiB against 40.0 GiB).

## 261112.005 (r269, release) -- the Meta split copy's tail guard is zeroed per device, so Qwen3.8-Flash-Next UD-Q4_K_XL stops emitting repeated `/` on a 3-GPU tensor split

**Release:** `v17-a691b2345-r269` -- 17 blocks (block 00 + blocks 01-16), base `a691b2345`, canonical tip
**`42f90c9b83812fb008c34045d6ad7260c2a690c4`**, tree **`db3494fa1d0a52aff7d6365f25067ee87848559b`**.  Blocks 09
and 10 amended (the generic/synchronous tail guard in 09, the segmented async tail in 10); no new block, no new
environment variable, no default moved, no real expert byte changed -- only the guard bytes after the last used
expert are now deterministic instead of uninitialised.

### The report

A `llama-server` on 3 x R9700 (gfx1201) with **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next UD-Q4_K_XL** (4 shards, 106 GiB),
`--n-cpu-moe 44 --limit-host-experts auto --split-mode tensor`, q8_0 K/V, `-c 204800`, adaptive MTP + ngram-mod,
returned **512 `/` characters** for the reporter's Three.js prompt; a simple "The capital of France is" request
returned `333333...`.  Q4_K_M and IQ4_NL of the same model were coherent, and `-sm layer` was coherent with the
same Q4_K_XL file.

### Root cause

`copy_experts` appends a `min(expert_size, 512)`-byte guard from the next expert after a used-expert group so the
quantized `MUL_MAT_ID` MMQ's tail over-read is finite (`ggml/src/ggml-backend.cpp`).  Under `-sm tensor`,
`ggml_backend_meta_set_tensor_async()` splits that contiguous source range across the devices.  For an axis-0
`ffn_down_exps` table the device slice is a row slice: Q8_0's 640-element row is **680 B**, and the 3-device
granularity-rounded shares are 128/256/256 elements = **136/272/272 B**.  The 512-byte guard is distributed as
136 + 272 + 104: the last device's slice starts at byte 408 and only 104 of its 272 bytes were written.  The other
168 bytes stayed uninitialised (normally NaN), and the MMQ over-read poisoned the tile -- the repeated-`/`
signature.

Q4_K_XL is the only quant here with **Q8_0 down experts** (layers 4, 30, 46, 47; layer 2 is Q5_K gate/up and is
not host-resident at `-ncmoe 44`).  Q4_K_M's down experts are Q5_1: 400-byte rows, so the 512-byte guard covers a
whole device row and the residual is absent.  The cache is not the culprit: `MOE_EXPERT_CACHE_MIB=0` still emits
`/`, and `GGML_META_SPLIT_COPY=0` (mirroring the per-split copy) is coherent.

### What shipped

* **Block 09** -- in the partial-tail path of the generic async setter, after the available source bytes are
  copied, zero the rest of each device's next-expert guard: the first 512 bytes of the **device slot**, filled
  contiguously (an axis-0 device row can be narrower than 512 B, so the fill spans device rows).  The head partial
  row is untouched; its prefix belongs to a previous chunk.  The synchronous segmented setter gets the same fill.
* **Block 10** -- the same fill in the segmented **async** setter (the fused-table path).
* No new variable, no default change.  The guard is still finite; it is now deterministic zero rather than
  whatever the previous allocation held.

## 261112.003 (r267, release) -- `v17-a691b2345-r267`: the node budget pays for the quantized KV cache's per-layer Hadamard rotation, so phi-4 loads again (GitHub issue #302)

**Release:** `v17-a691b2345-r267` -- 17 blocks (block 00 + blocks 01-16), base `a691b2345`, canonical tip

### The report (GitHub issue #302, `overdoingism`)

`phi-4-IQ4_XS.gguf` (`phi3` arch, 40 layers, 243 tensors) on 1x R9700 aborts ~3 s into
`llama-server -c 16384 -ub 2048 -b 2048 -fa on -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -ngl 99 --parallel 1` with a bare
`ggml-impl.h:337: fatal error` ("visited all hash table entries") at
`ggml_abort <- ggml_backend_sched_split_graph <- llama_context::graph_reserve <- resolve_fused_ops::{lambda} <-
sched_reserve`.  Their matrix: `f16`/`f16` loads (FA on or off), `q8_0`/`q8_0` aborts on r244 and r251 and loads on
r235, and Qwen3.x / Mistral-7B / gpt-oss-20b load with the same q8_0 KV.  They had not checked whether mainline
`a691b2345` alone aborts, and suspected the 1944-entry `graph_max_nodes()` budget.

### What it is (upstream's regression, reproduced here on unmodified mainline)

Upstream **#31073** (`kv-cache: keep rotation constants on the GPU`, between r235's base `6050b467c` and r244's
`a691b2345`) made the KV Hadamard rotation constants **per layer on the device**; before it, one K and one V constant
were shared by the whole graph.  A quantized K/V cache rotates Q/K/V/out per layer through four
`llama_mul_mat_hadamard` chains (3 nodes each) and now carries **2 leafs per layer** too: **12 nodes + 2 leafs = 14
hash entries per layer**.  phi-4's graph is **2064 entries (1649 nodes + 415 leafs)** against a
`graph_max_nodes()` budget of `max(1024, 8*243) = 1944` (hash table 2053) -- short by 11, and `f16` is 1169 nodes,
which is why only the quantized arm died.  Verified by graph census (`HIST`/`DUMP` instrumentation, removed before
the commit): the q8_0 graph carries 321 `MUL_MAT` / 320 `RESHAPE` / 40 `CONT` / 40 `FLASH_ATTN_EXT` against the f16
arm's 161 / 40 / 0 / 40, and the extra nodes are `cache_k_rot_l0` / `cache_v_rot_l0` mul-mats.

**Mainline `a691b2345` alone aborts on the same model, at the same bare abort.**  Built unpatched in a separate tree
(`all` target, gfx1201): its own **`kv_rows` probe** (`n_tokens=1`, `split_only`) splits a **1649-node / 413-leaf**
graph against the same 2053-entry table and dies at `ggml_hash_find_or_insert`.  So there is no assert to see: the
`split_only` probe path calls `ggml_backend_sched_split_graph` directly, which is also why the delivery's own probe
(derived-kq-mask, 64 tokens, the same node count) reaches it with no llama-level message.  Which probe dies is the
only difference: upstream probes `kv_rows` unconditionally, the delivery only with `--kv-unified`, so on
`llama-cli`'s default the derived-kq-mask probe is the one that overflows.

### What shipped

* `llama_memory_i::graph_extra_entries()` -- the graph entries a memory module's own builders add every graph --
  implemented by `llama_kv_cache` (**7 per rotated tensor per layer**: 6 nodes + 1 leaf) by walking the rotation
  tensors `init_rotation()` really created, and summed by `llama_kv_cache_iswa` / `llama_memory_hybrid` /
  `llama_memory_hybrid_iswa`.  No copy of the ctor's predicate (quantized type, `n_embd_head_%64`, `LLAMA_ATTN_ROT_DISABLE`).
* `graph_max_nodes()` adds that term.  phi-4 **1944 -> 2504** (hash table 2053 -> 4099), gemma-4-12B (iSWA, both
  sub-caches) **6016**.  Host-only cost: the scheduler's context buffer is `graph_size * 30 * 2 * sizeof(ggml_tensor)`
  (phi-4 47 -> 60 MB); nothing on the device moves.
* `ggml_graph_n_leafs()` (the id arrays are indexed over nodes AND leafs).
* `graph_reserve()` **refuses** a graph that does not fit and names both numbers (`needs 2064 entries (1649 nodes +
  415 leafs) but the scheduler was reserved for 1944`) instead of aborting inside ggml, and `resolve_fused_ops()`
  treats an unreservable probe graph as "cannot verify -> disable" instead of throwing.

## 261112.001 (r266) -- block 16's prefill band is served IN PLACE out of the arena: the residents are no longer D2D-copied every pass (`TODO` #317 CLOSED)

**Change (block-16 amendment, one file, no new block, no new env).**  The arena tier is only partly resident, so
above the decode/verify band it staged the used expert set into the scheduler's split-input copy -- D2D-copying
every *resident* used expert out of the store on every pass, i.e. `O(used)` instead of `O(missing)`: 30 060.5 MiB of
copy against 1 578.6 MiB read for a 38-token prompt (r265's census).  The prefill band now takes the slot-remap path
the identity tier got in r258: admit the used-and-missing experts, publish the slot-remapped ids for the whole
`n_used x n_tok` routing, and let `MUL_MAT_ID` read the arena.  `MOE_EXPERT_PREFILL_IN_PLACE` is the knob (it already
existed); `=0` is byte-identical to r265.

**Policy, as the maintainer decided it** (Q1 = rank by the decode heat prior; Q2 = prefer room, then "it should
never evict", refined by the maintainer to "may admit if there is room").  Admission is a three-rung ladder: an
**empty** slot; one of the table's **landing** slots (the coldest `slots/16` seeded slots -- a SOFT reserve, so a
cold start pays no extra read and decode keeps them until prefill needs them); then a resident **this pass does not
route**, coldest by the decode heat prior.  A slot the pass ROUTES is never reclaimed -- that is also the arm's hard
bound (`|used| <= slots`), and a table that cannot be served in place is declined WHOLE (no partial admission) and
keeps the scheduler's copy.

in every arm):

**The wide band is conditional and that is structural**: a 4 096-token ubatch routes all 256 experts of a trunk
table, so it can only be served in place when the arena holds that many slots -- at `-lme 90` (200 slots) the Q8 arm
declines 123 of 144 tables and is exactly inert (3 paired runs, 0 difference); at `-lme 75` (241) it serves most
calls; at `-ub 1024` both serve partially and gain.  Nothing regresses when it declines.

**Decode cost, answered by simulation and then by measurement.**  A new `--prefill-admit {off,free,landing,ladder}`
model in `scripts/moe-trace-replay.py` (driver `archive/work/prefill-in-place-arena/admission-sim.sh`) replays a real
Q8_0 `-lme 90` trace (538-token prompt + 200 decode tokens): the ladder **removes 447-1101 of 11 559 decode misses**
(prompt 200-538 tokens, `h` 0.9515 -> 0.9534-0.9561) -- admitting on prefill is a PREFETCH of the continuation, not a
disturbance, and the reclaim rung only gives up the coldest non-routed expert.  The reserve-only arm (the strict
"never evict" reading) is the worst of the four: it declines 99-138 of 144 tables and is neutral-to-negative.

**Two-slot server interference -- a NEW gate, because this was omitted too long.**  `scripts/gate-prefill-server-slots.sh`
(+ `.py`) runs `llama-server -np 2` and fires request B's 250-token PREFILL into the middle of request A's DECODE:

A's longest inter-token gap **414 vs 623 ms**, B 15.6 s vs 17.2 s, no faults, and the admission really ran
(126 calls x 78 tables, 1959 admissions).

**A real defect the logits gate caught** (recorded so it cannot come back): the first cut checked "never reclaim a
