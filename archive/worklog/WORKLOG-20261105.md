## 261105.019 (campaign, no delivery change) -- write-back vs page-cache warmer, priced

The maintainer's question: an admitted expert's arena victim is dropped and re-read from storage; why not
write it back, and (with mmap) why not at least warm the dropped expert into the page cache?

* **The description is correct**, and the delivery does drop the victim (no write-back) — in the complement
  case the pool does not hold it either.
* **Why r113's refusal does not cover this case**: its `pool-swap-slot` arm had a pool that *covered* the
  complement (0 MiB of L3 to win) and ran `-sm tensor`, where the copy is a 2-D strided
  `cudaMemcpy2DAsync` (6 259 swaps).  The complement-scarce single-device case is 1-D and the replay says it
  removes ~65 % of the L3 at 1.55 copies per MiB; and a write-back is **slot-neutral** (the promoted expert
  vacated the pool slot the victim takes), so it needs no extra memory.
* **The warmer was modelled and measured** (`--evict-warm` in the replayer): **-20.5 % / -15.7 % / -15.0 %**
  of the storage-read traffic at 19 GiB/50 %, 19 GiB/40 %, 10 GiB/50 %, with **zero device copies**.
* **The distinction that decides it**: a write-back *eliminates* the read and so saves **bandwidth**; a warmer
  only moves the read's **latency** and saves no bandwidth, so on a bandwidth-bound box it competes with the
  critical path.  The warmer is right for a big box where a miss was already cheap; the write-back is the one
  that helps the memory-tight box the campaign cares about.  Neither exists in the delivery.

## 261105.015 (campaign, no delivery change) -- `wip/host-expert-split/` step 1: the trace, the replay, two replayer fixes, and the answer to "is a swap worth it"

**No delivery change.**  `patches/`, `release.json` and the tag are untouched.

* **The trace** (one bounded `llama-cli` run, 1 R9700, no all-reduce, no drafter, 83 s) is in
  `wip/host-expert-split/STEP1-REPLAY.md` with the full grid.  Model calibration: `h` 0.6117 against the run's
  own turn counter 0.6300.
* **Two replayer defects found and fixed** (`scripts/moe-trace-replay.py`, `scripts/README.md`):
  (a) `--pool-init sidecar-complement` leaves slots EMPTY when the budget exceeds the arena's complement, which
  the shipped re-rank never does — the new **`pool-layout`** policy models the delivery (heat-seeded arena, pool
  re-laid out as complement + mirrored residents at the first decode pass) and reproduces the measured mirror
  (0.00 MiB/token of L3, as `wip/bench-56gb/RESULTS.md` measured); `pool-none` read 16.6 for the same budget.
  (b) the pool table's copy columns are now per-token from the tool's own decode-band denominator — dividing by
  the trace's total token count inflated every "MiB saved per MiB copied" ratio ~5.7x and inverted a ranking
  for an hour of this session.
* **The answer.**  A swap is worth it **exactly where an arena miss would be expensive**: it removes ~1 MiB of
  L3 per 1.55 MiB of extra PCIe copy (0.65 MiB saved per MiB copied), so on a 56 GiB disk-bound box it is
  decisive and on a page-cached box it is ~+9 %.  At a budget that covers the complement it removes nothing and
  costs 5-6 MiB/token: r113 stands, and so does PR #267's own -3 %.
* **The maintainer's periodic conditional swap** (every N rounds, coldest arena vs hottest pool, only if
  hotter; K per round) was modelled as `--rebalance`: it is **4-5x less copy-efficient than the on-demand sink**
  (0.12-0.18 against 0.65), K=4 is *worse* than K=1, and converted to time it is **negative on a page-cached
  box**.  The guard is doing real work (guard off costs 3.3x the copies); the counter-transfer subtlety is not
  (`carry`/`reset`/`prior` are identical on this trace, because after decay every resident's count sits at 0-1).
  Reported, not adopted.
* **Next**: no C1 until the open question in `STEP1-REPLAY.md` §5.4 is answered — our arena holds 93.5 % at 43 %
  of the set while a 50 %-of-the-set prior-ranked complement answers only 56 % of its misses, where the
  coverage arithmetic predicts ~88 %.  If that is the prior being weak, the fix is `pool_rerank_from_heat_locked`
  and a demote buys little; if it is the replay's arena drift, the demote is worth building.  That is one
  measurement on the 56 GiB rig, and it decides the campaign.

* **@briansp2020** again -- a correction that it IS specific to the take-over path, with the decline patch.

### What was actually wrong (two defects, one misleading message, one platform ceiling)

1. **A fill failure inside the call did not decline that call** (confirmed by reading the code, then measured):
   `access_locked` rolls the failed admission back and sets `t.fill_failed`, but the guard in
   `moe_cache_update_host` sits at the top of the function.  The failing call continued: pass 2 found a cold or
   pool id for the failed expert (a pool HIT is rolled back as an arena admission but is still pool-resident),
   `all` stayed true, and the hook returned TRUE -- the scheduler skipped `input_cpy`, while
   `moe_cache_get_table`, which the consuming op calls a few lines later, saw the same flag and returned false.
   The op read a buffer nobody filled.  Fix: re-check `t.fill_failed` right after pass 1, before anything is
   remapped, and add `!t.fill_failed` to `devremap_ok_locked` so the one-call-late remap follows the same rule.
2. **The failure message named a synthesized device error.**  `expert_host_src` failing is a host-side answer
   (no pool slot, no pinned bounce, no mapping) yet the caller substituted `cudaErrorInvalidDeviceFunction`,
   which printed `invalid device function`.  Replaced by a named `k_no_host_source` sentinel that is never
   passed to `cudaGetErrorString`, plus a real reason per failure (`no pinned host source (<why>)`).
3. **A refused pinned allocation was retried forever.**  On Windows a `hipHostMalloc` refusal surfaces as
   `hipErrorLaunchFailure` and latches process-wide (`ROCM-33542` / rocm-systems#14578, relayed in
   ROCm/TheRock#9339), so the per-table bounce `cudaHostAlloc` retry turned one refusal into thousands of
   failures.  The first refusal now logs once with the driver's string and latches the pinned tier OFF.
4. **The pool was sized against `MemAvailable` on a platform whose pin ceiling is not `MemAvailable`.**
   WDDM charges pinned host pages against the adapter's shared segment once per visible adapter, so the ceiling
   is ~`(total RAM / 2) / visible adapters`: ~23 GiB on the reporter's 47 GB box against the ~26.6 GiB pool
   `MOE_HOST_POOL_RAM_PCT=75` planned -- the tail pools and then every bounce entry are refused and the tier
   collapses to the pageable path.  A ceiling derived from that model was implemented, measured to be
   unverifiable here, and **withdrawn on the maintainer's call** (a heuristic that cannot be measured would cap
   a healthy box): the shipped guard is the latch in (3) plus a census line, and `TODO.md` carries "measure the
   aperture segment's `CommitLimit` before adding a ceiling".
5. **The 40 GB "private commit" is very likely the reported column, not commit.**  A `PAGE_READONLY`
   `MapViewOfFile` is file-backed: the delivery copies nothing at load (host buffer over the mapping,
   `mark_mmap_used` only), so the only touches are the pool/bounce fill memcpys and the scheduler fallback --
   whose byte count the census already prints.  Asked the reporter to check Task Manager's `Committed` /
   RAMMap's mapped-file pages; no code change (INVARIANTS r223 fact 5).

## 261105.011 (r222 tooling, no delivery change) -- the container images name their RELEASE, not only the fork point (issue #272 follow-up)

**No delivery change.**  `patches/`, `release.json` and the artefact hashes are untouched (the release stays
`v16-6050b467c-r222`, tip `80bfb80be`, tree `d9495be3`); this is `scripts/build-containers.sh`, the Dockerfile's
labels and `CONTAINERS.md`.

### Why

@hermeticvm confirmed the r218 fix for issue #272 on his RX 6700 XT (both DSpark replay arms pass, plus a real
six-task agent run) and added the one thing he could not do: attribute the image he tested.  His words: "labels
identify fork point `6050b467c` but not the patch release ... I cannot attest it is exactly r218 or r219".
That was true and it was our defect, in two layers:

* the GHCR tags were fork-point only (`server-rocm-10.1-6050b467c`), and the moving tags (`rocm-10.1`,
  `latest`) are overwritten on every release, so the fork-point tag is the only stable handle -- and one fork
  point serves every release on it;
* `build-containers.sh` passed `APP_VERSION=base` and `APP_REVISION=base`, so even
  `org.opencontainers.image.version` / `.revision` named the fork point twice, and nothing in the image
  identified the release.

### What changed

* every target also gets a `<tag>-<release>` tag (`full|light|server-rocm-<line>-v<blocks>-<base>-r<N>`, and a
  `rocm-<line>-<release>` alias for the server image).  The moving and fork-point tags stay for compatibility,
  and they are what the GHCR retention keep-rule matches, so the prune is unaffected: one push is one manifest

## 261105.009 (r222) -- issue #275: the VMM capability probe ran in a FOREIGN driver context, which on Windows kills the first kernel launch of a plain 2-GPU `-sm tensor` run (block 15)

**Released as `v16-6050b467c-r222` (16 blocks).**  Fork point unchanged (`6050b467c`), canonical tip
**`80bfb80bed3e1752910aa82257404adb7b3894e8`**, tree **`d9495be3ba2fbfbd601e8740d1da2d759b9bc256`**.
Block 15 amended; blocks 00-14 keep identical deltas (their `.patch` files change only in the `From <sha>`

### The report

`jagalves` (Windows 11/Server, 2 x R9700 gfx1201, ROCm 10.1 Windows dist): a PLAIN 2-GPU `-sm tensor` run dies
at the first kernel launch with an access violation in `hipHccModuleLaunchKernel`, no stdout, both device
orders; 1 GPU unaffected; bisected r203 PASS / r204 FAIL, i.e. the release that added the Meta
`ggml_backend_meta_kv_region_buft` hook (`TODO` #124).  No window and no pool is asked for
(`LLAMA_KV_RESIDENT_CELLS` unset, so `kv_resident_cells == 0`), and `LLAMA_KV_REGION=0` makes it pass -- which
points at the one piece of driver traffic the plain path still does: the CAPABILITY PROBE.

### Reproduced on Linux, and what the defect actually is

The crash needs the Windows/HCC context handling, so Linux does not fault -- but the wrong context is
reproducible and was measured directly.  With a temporary `cudaGetDevice()` print at the top of
`ggml_cuda_vmm_region_available` (r221 tree, `HIP_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0,2 -sm tensor`):

```
VMMPROBE device=0 current_context=1 cross=1
VMMPROBE device=1 current_context=1 cross=0
```

Device 0's probe -- `cuMemAddressReserve`, `cuMemCreate`/`Map`/`SetAccess`, `cuMemUnmap`,
`cuMemAddressFree`, plus the quarantine `cudaMalloc`/`cudaFree` -- runs its whole round trip in **device 1's
context**, because `llama_kv_cache` init asks each child of the Meta device in turn (via
`ggml_backend_meta_kv_region_buft(kv_dev, 1)`) and nothing in the chain binds the device.  `alloc_buffer`
does bind it; the probe was the one path that did not.  That is exactly the class of traffic the reporter
identified, and the reason it is invisible on 1 GPU (there is only one context to be on).

### The fix (block 15, three parts)

1. **Device discipline in the provider.**  Every entry point that can reach the driver binds the region's
   device first (`vmm_bind_device` -> `ggml_cuda_set_device`): `create`, `free`, `map_span`, `ensure_span`,
   `clear`, `dump`, `available`, `selftest`, and the `cuMemGetAllocationGranularity` query.  Binding in the
   module rather than at each call site is the invariant -- a new caller cannot forget it, and `free` (which
   unmaps and releases VA from a context destructor) had the same latent defect.
2. **A buffer type may not touch the driver while it is constructed.**  New
   `ggml_cuda_vmm_region_capable(device)` answers from the two facts the backend latched at init
   (`devices[].vmm` + its RECOMMENDED granularity) with no driver traffic, and
   `ggml_backend_cuda_kv_region_buffer_type_split` now asks THAT.  This is the reporter's option 2, and it is
   the part that fixes the crash: the plain `-sm tensor` path now performs **no VMM driver call at all** (the
   `VMM region available` line is gone from the 2-GPU arm).
3. **The real probe moved to allocation time.**  `alloc_buffer` (which already bound the device) runs
   `ggml_cuda_vmm_region_available` once, before `create`, and still falls back to the plain device buffer
   with the same warning -- so "reserves but refuses a one-unit map" is still caught BEFORE any KV byte is
   committed, one step later than before and in the right context.  The reporter's objection to option 2
   (the `LLAMA_LOG_INFO` line moves) is answered: the refusal now happens at alloc, the fallback is
   unchanged.

### Also fixed on the way (both in block 15's files)

* **The selftest's verdict was invisible.**  `ENVIRONMENT.md` has said "must print PASS, and must print FAIL
  with `LLAMA_KV_REGION_QUARANTINE=0`" since r178, but the verdict was a `GGML_LOG_INFO` line and
  `common_log_get_verbosity()` maps INFO to TRACE -- so at every default BOTH verdicts were suppressed, and a
  FAIL was as quiet as a PASS.  Two more layers hide it in `llama-cli`: its default verbosity is error-only,
  and the `--fit` probe wraps the logger so anything below its own minimum becomes DEBUG.  The verdict is now
  WARN (PASS) / ERROR (FAIL), so a failure can no longer pass a gate silently; a PASS is visible with `-v`
  (which `scripts/gate-kv-residency.sh` passes) and `ENVIRONMENT.md` says so.
* **`scripts/make-patches.sh` silently re-cut the PREVIOUS set.**  Its default tip comes from
  `release.json`, so running it after amending a block (without updating `release.json` first) regenerated
  the old patch series, printed "Regenerated 16 patches" and exited 0 while the amendment was nowhere in the
  delivery -- this session hit it and only a `grep` of the patch found it.  It now refuses when the fork is
  checked out at a tip that is not `release.json.tip` and no tip was passed explicitly.
* **`scripts/gate-kv-residency.sh` now gates the provider's selftest**: `wselftest` must PASS and
  `wselftest0` (`LLAMA_KV_REGION_QUARANTINE=0`, the known-bad state) must FAIL, so the oracle is proven
  sensitive on every run instead of on trust.  The selftest was previously a documented-but-ungated

### What shipped: the copy-event fix (block 12)

The diagnosis is the valuable half and it is **not** about two lanes: the scheduler's cross-backend
split-input copy recorded the backend's **shared** per-device `copy_event` and waited the destination
stream on it, and a queued HIP stream wait resolves against the **latest** record at execution -- so a copy
still in flight re-records the event and re-binds the consumers already queued on it, serializing
independent copies.  That is reachable in this delivery wherever two copies overlap (the pipelined layer
split does it).  `cpy_tensor_async`'s body is now `cpy_tensor_async_impl(..., ev_nowait)`; the new optional
iface member `cpy_tensor_async_nowait` takes a **caller-owned** event, recorded on the source stream right
behind the copy and waited on by the consumer, so no wait is ever re-bound.  The scheduler keeps a ring of
those events per source backend (a slot is re-recorded only after its previous record completed) and
restarts the rings at every full synchronize.  `GGML_SCHED_COPY_EVENT=shared` restores the old path (kill
switch, A/B and bisection; `ENVIRONMENT.md` §2).

**Bit-exact and throughput-neutral** (pp8192 3-GPU `-sm layer`: 6311 vs 6317 t/s): the repair decouples
copies, it is not a speedup where they were not contending -- and on this box they were not.

## 261105.005 (r219) -- issue #274: on Windows the host pool's fills could not be serviced, and a failed fill was served as resident (block 12)

**Released as `v16-6050b467c-r219` (16 blocks; block 12 amended).**  Fork point unchanged (`6050b467c`),
canonical tip **`2f7761bf5`**, tree **`d9a6468856304e9cf81fbfebcfe5c9366ec10c26`**.  Reported by **@DanoPTT**
([issue #274](https://github.com/stew675/llama-cpp-rdna-boosts/issues/274)) on **Windows 11 / ROCm 10.0**, one
R9700, 47.3 GB RAM, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next GSQ-RCO IQ3_XXS with `--n-cpu-moe 47 --host-experts pool --load-mode
mmap`: a 4996-token prompt prefilled at **20.4 t/s**, `MoE arena = 0.4939`, and greedy generation stopped after
**one token**.  Every diagnostic below is reproducible with the two new diagnostic knobs, which is the point:
this box has no Windows toolchain (`TODO` #95), so the platform state and the forced failure are now *arms*.

### What was wrong (two defects, and the reporter's log named both)

1. **The pool's DISK TIER was treated as required.**  A fill has two possible sources: the read-only model
   mapping (a `memcpy` out of the page cache -- always present, because a pool's master *is* that mapping) and
   the pool's disk tier (`MOE_HOST_POOL_DIO=1`, `pread`/`O_DIRECT`).  The file I/O is a **compile-time stub on
   Windows** (`pool_open_file` -> -1, the issue-#130 portability shape), so with the disk tier selected every
   pool fill failed, `pool_fill_empty_locked` marked the pool `failed`, and `table_pool_locked()` returned null
   for the rest of the run -- the pool "never appeared".  The per-table bounce fallback then asked for the same
   missing tier and returned false: `fill_slot_from_host: no pinned host source for layer=37 ... (pool and
   bounce both unavailable)`.  **The reporter reached the disk tier by default**: r209's `MOE_HOST_POOL_DIO=auto`
   chose `O_DIRECT` whenever `pool + host set > MemAvailable` (30 GB + 40 GB against 38 GB), which is exactly
   their box.  r212 has since made `auto` mirror `--load-mode` (a mapped load fills buffered), so the default
   path into this is gone -- but the mode is one env var away and the failure was silent corruption.
2. **A fill that failed was recorded as RESIDENT.**  `access_locked()` registered `t.expert_slot[expert]` and
   returned the arena pointer *before* the copy, so a failed fill left the slot serving stale bytes: the arena
   "hit rate" (`h`) still looked plausible, the text was garbage, and the only trace was
   `access_locked: async fill failed table layer=1 role=blk.1.ffn_gate_exps.weight expert=320: no error` --
   `cudaGetLastError()` is empty for a host-side failure, so the message hid the cause.  The reporter guessed
   this half of it ("the fill also still returns the slot as resident") and was right.
3. **The decisions were invisible.**  Every line that would have explained the state (`host experts:` tier
   census, `host pool enabled`, the per-table `host pool (...)` build line, the fill mode) is `GGML_LOG_INFO`,
   and `common_log_get_verbosity()` maps INFO to `LOG_LEVEL_TRACE` against a default threshold of
   `LOG_LEVEL_INFO`: at the default verbosity an operator sees **only** WARN and above, i.e. every failure and
   none of the decisions.  That is why a pool that had *failed* looked like a pool that was never requested.

### The fix (block 12, no new default, no behaviour change where the disk tier exists)

* The disk tier is **best-effort everywhere**: `pool_fill_slot_locked()` falls back to the mapping when the
  file cannot be opened or a read fails (it must not fail the pool), `pool_mirror_bulk_fill_locked()`'s failure
  now falls through to the ranked per-slot path, `expert_host_src()`'s bounce ring falls back to the mapping
  instead of returning false, and `pool_dio_effective()` **refuses** a requested `O_DIRECT` on a platform with
  no pool file I/O, with one WARN.  The pool, its pin and the in-place cold read are untouched.
* A failed fill **rolls the admission back** (no `expert_slot` entry, the fill counters undone), the census
  reports `FAILED fills: N`, and the message carries the real cause (`cudaGetErrorString(err)`, once per table
  plus every 10000th).  The table is then marked `fill_failed` and **stands down wholesale**
  (`moe_cache_update_host`, `moe_cache_get_table`) -- the same policy as `alloc_failed` -- so the scheduler
  serves the op from the host master instead of the arena.  The stand-down is *part of the fix*, not a
  belt-and-braces: with the rollback alone, the eager take-over still emitted pool-cold ids for a table whose
  arena was empty and the arm produced garbage (the first cut measured `b7e2732392540006`, i.e. wrong).
* `report_host_expert_placement()` is **WARN** (a one-off decision line, the same class as
  `MoE expert cache (auto): arena ...`) and carries the pool-source count (`141/141 pool sources registered`),
  which is the first number a "the pool never appeared" report needs.

### What was wrong

The third turn of their replay answered `HTTP 500 decode() failed: failed to process speculative batch` with
`inconsistent sequence positions ... sequence 0 ... X = 1995 ... starting position of Y = 2242`; n-gram drafting
alone, `cache_prompt: false` and `-ctxcp 0` were all clean, and their upstream control was clean.  **The cause is
this delivery's DFlash prompt-ingest tail (r130, block 12) — and it is not RDNA2-specific.**  `process()` ingests
only the last `n_swa` (2048) rows of the prompt, which is exact (§53) but assumes the draft's cache stays
contiguous with the rows it does ingest.  A prefix kept from an earlier task breaks that as soon as a turn adds
more than `n_swa` tokens: the new window starts past the draft's kept rows, so the draft's cache ends at the
reused prefix while its first ingested row is at `prompt_end - n_swa`.  A **strict** (non-M-RoPE) draft — the
`NORM`/`NEOX` backbone class that DSpark drafts with no `dflash.rope.dimension_sections` use — has its batch
**rejected**, and the request 500s.  An **M-RoPE** draft is allowed the forward jump and instead ingests a batch
whose first rows attend to nothing.  The MTP drafter has the same exposure through `LLAMA_MTP_INGEST_TAIL`
(block 02, r118).  The reporter's arithmetic fits exactly: 246 rows skipped, `Y = prompt_end - 2048`,
`X + 1 = 1996` = the "1996 cached tokens" their upstream control reported.

### The fix (78 lines, no new knob, no default change)

`spec_draft_align_cache()` (`common/speculative.cpp`, added by block 02 and reused by block 12's DFlash/DSpark
drafter) is called by both `process()` implementations before the injection batch is built.  A draft **behind**
the batch drops its stale prefix for a strict draft — every row of it is older than the window of the prompt's
end, so no later row can attend to it, and the batch that would have carried it is gone — and starts a fresh
window at the first ingested row, exactly as it does for a first-ever prompt; an M-RoPE draft keeps the jump it
already tolerated, so that path is unchanged.  A draft **ahead** of the batch (a checkpoint restore can put it
past the position the task resumes from) is trimmed in every case, because both batch rules reject that overlap.
A `gemma4`-style cache shared with the target is never touched.

### 1. The two grids (block 10: `fattn-common.cuh`, `norm.cu`)

`flash_attn_stream_k_fixup_uniform`'s tile grid and `rms_norm_scale_pair_f32`'s `2*nrows*nchannels` grid
now go through `ggml_cuda_grid_stall_pad` and add the returning block, exactly as mmvq/mmvf/MoE do:
a guard in each kernel and one extra block at each launch.  **The stall reproduces on this box**
(`archive/work/rdna4-grid-stall-pad/graphgap.hip` extended to large grids): 256 blocks 12.63 us against
257 blocks 2.34, 512 13.63 against 513 2.71, 1024 11.62 against 1025 3.32.  **But it is a fixed ~10 us and
only dominates a short kernel**: from ~2048 blocks on (16384 against 16385 = 24.41 against 24.49) there is
nothing to recover.  A rocprofv3 trace of the reporter's model shape shows both grids *do* hit the
condition but at 16384 / 32768-393216 blocks on this model's row and channel counts, so here the pad is
neutral and the gain is theirs (256-block grids: 15.48 -> 10.08 us and 15.8 -> 2.4 us per launch).

### 2. `seq_rm` with an idle slot parked (block 12: `llama-kv-cache.cpp`)

`llama_kv_cache::seq_rm()` scanned `[used_min(), used_max_p1())`; with `--kv-unified --parallel 2` the
parked idle slot sits at the top of the cache, so `used_max_p1()` is the cache size and every speculative
step scanned all 245 760 cells.  A parked sequence holds only cells at or above `park_bound()`, so a
sequence that is **not** parked now stops the scan there (`used_max_p1_below(park_bound())`).  Measured
with a public-API harness on a 131 072-cell unified cache, 4096 live cells, a 64-cell parked sequence:
**42.127 -> 1.358 us per call** (31x), and the parked sequence's cells, a full removal of the live sequence
and the unpark are all unchanged.  `scripts/gate-park-idle-slots.sh` is green (6 park moves, 2 unpark,
park == lazyoff on all four requests, parkoff still differs).

### 3. The drafter's prompt is no longer copied every step (block 12: `server-context.cpp`)

Without media the slot's own token vector *is* the prompt the drafters read inside
`common_speculative_draft()`, so `slot.spec_prompt` is only filled when `has_mtmd`.  The removed work is
2.8 us/step at 10.5K tokens and **28.8 us/step at 110K** (isolated measurement of the same copy).

The reporter's item 4 (`LLAMA_RS_ROLLBACK_DEBUG` read on every recurrent rollback) was one case of a class.

## 261105.002 (r217) -- the KV residency gate now asserts the pool's PROMPT rate: a placement may not be correct and slow

**Tooling only: `scripts/gate-kv-residency.sh` + its two index entries.  `patches/` is unchanged and the release
set stays `v16-6050b467c-r215`.**

`261105.001` found block 15's attendance pool at -48 % prompt throughput while every gate assertion passed.  The
gate was not wrong -- it asserts text identity and residency, and that is its job -- but nothing in the release
flow looked at the *speed* of a placement, so a correct-and-slow placement passed every gate and shipped.  The
arms already run one 25 763-token prompt each with the same binary on the same device, so the comparison was
sitting in the logs unread.

**What it asserts now.**  In the one-device pool section, `pres` must keep `PREFILL_MIN_PCT` (default **40 %**)
of `pref`'s `prompt eval` tokens/s.  Both numbers are parsed from the arms' own llama-cli perf line
(`prompt_ts()`), so there is no second measurement and no extra runtime; a missing rate is a FAIL rather than a
skip, because a silently vacuous assertion is the failure mode this gate exists to refuse.  `pfloor` runs with
`LLAMA_KV_POOL_VERIFY=1` and is explicitly not a timing arm.  The **window** arms' rates are printed and not
asserted: a window below the depth the run reaches is a documented 11-26 % trade-off (`ENVIRONMENT.md` 17.0),
and gating it would fight a deliberate design.

**Why the floor is 40 % and not 80 %.**  It is a *regression* floor, not a target: the pool's known cost must
pass.  The analogue measurement (`261105.001`, IQ3_S, `-c 204800`) read 427.5 against 829.3 t/s = 52 %, and the
canonical gate arms carry a smaller quant, a 2x smaller context and therefore a larger arena, so they are
expected at or above ~50 %.  40 % passes the known behaviour and still fails a further regression.  The script
header and the assertion's comment both say that a change which legitimately moves the ratio must move this
default **and** this entry together -- never widen it silently to get green.

**Validation.**

* `--window-only` end to end on this box (Qwen3.5-9B in place of the absent 4B default): all 10 residency/text
  assertions PASS, and the new INFO line reads `wref 3702.05 / wres 3957.52 / wspill 2405.31 t/s` -- the spill
  cost as an observation, not a failure, which is the intended behaviour of the non-asserted arm.
* The assertion itself, graded through `REUSE=1` on real perf lines: identical arms PASS; `pres` at 24 % of
  `pref` FAILs with the message above; a log with the perf line stripped FAILs as vacuous.  One FAIL per cause.
* The canonical pool arms **cannot run on a 59 GiB host**: `-lm none -lzm off` makes the 26.8 GiB PLE resident
  beside the ~44 GiB host expert set (~72 GiB).  Attempting it on this box drove `MemAvailable` to 0 and had to
  be killed; it is now stated in the script header, `MANIFESTS.md` gate 17 and `scripts/README.md`, and the
  guidance is to point `MODEL_POOL`/`DRAFT_POOL`/`PROMPT_POOL` elsewhere or grade `--window-only`.  The
  40 % default is therefore set from the IQ3_S analogue, not from the canonical arms -- recorded here so nobody
  later mistakes it for a measured value of those arms.
* `-h` now prints the whole header (`2,107p`, it stopped at `2,89p` and silently dropped `KEEPOUT`, `REUSE`
  and `ROCBLAS_USE_HIPBLASLT` from the usage text -- a pre-existing truncation, fixed here because the new
  variable is in the same block).

**Follow-up worth doing:** the pool section is the only place this class of regression is visible, so gate 17's
row in `MANIFESTS.md` is now the one that has to be run for any block-15 change; nothing else catches it.

## 261105.001 (r216) -- the attendance pool costs ~50 % of PROMPT throughput on a config whose KV already fits; the `runme` returns to a resident KV


**Docs only: `patches/` is unchanged and the release set stays `v16-6050b467c-r215` (tip `790686b53`, tree
`19200174f`).**  No block is amended; this entry records a *config* finding and the measurement behind it.

Reported symptom: the Qwen3.8-Flash-Next GSQ-RCO-IQ3_S `runme` "barely touches 400 t/s" prefill at `-c 204800`
on one RX 7900 XTX, while the same model and card read ~1 030 t/s at pp8K through `llama-bench` at a small
context.  Two causes, both configuration:

### 1. The file could not start on a 59 GiB host

`--limit-host-experts 54` (a hard GiB budget) is refused outright when the pinned master would exceed 90 % of
the memory available at load: `--limit-host-experts 54 (GiB) is more than 90 % of the available memory
(59 GiB)`.  It was written for a 62-64 GiB host and this one reports 59 GiB `MemAvailable`.  A later flag on
the command line cannot rescue that -- the refusal happens while the argument is parsed -- so the budget has
to be `auto`, which pins the whole 46.84 GiB host-expert set here (80 % of `MemAvailable`) and declines to
the bounded host pool when it does not fit.  **A fixed `--limit-host-experts N` is a landmine in any shared
config**: the same file starts on one box and refuses on a smaller one, with the reason buried in a usage
dump.

### 2. The pool's page-fill path is per DECODE step, so at PROMPT time it is pure overhead

`LLAMA_KV_REGION_POOL=16384` keeps the authoritative KV in a pinned host mirror and resolves the pages a
*decode step* attends to.  A prompt chunk writes every cell of its range, so the page-fill path runs over the
whole chunk with no reuse to pay it back.  On a config whose KV already fits, that is ~50 % of the prompt.

### The measured shape of the fix

One RX 7900 XTX (gfx1100, 24 GiB, PCIe 4.0 x16), Ryzen 9 7950X (16C, SMT off), 59 GiB `MemAvailable`, ROCm
10.1.0-gfx1100, llama.cpp `790686b53` (the r215 tree; binaries rebuilt for this measurement), `-c 204800`,
`-ncmoe 48`, q8_0 KV, the Q4_K_M MTP draft (`--spec-draft-n-max 3`), temp 0, one request per cell, median of
2 (one at 113K).  Prompt lengths 27 763 / 6 874 / 113 321 tokens:

| arm | pp32K | pp8K | pp113K | tg (192 tok) | MoE arena |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `LLAMA_KV_REGION_POOL=16384` (the file before this entry) | 427.5 | 463 | 409.2 | 59.4 | 12 084 MiB (25.2 %) |
| plain device KV (**new default**) | **829.3** | **769** | **808.2** | 52.8 | 9 742 MiB (20.3 %) |
| the same `+ LLAMA_KV_RESIDENT_CELLS=65536` | -- | 790.6 | 530.1 | 59.1 | 11 616 MiB (24.2 %) |

+94 % prefill at 32K, +66 % at 8K, +97 % at 113K, for -11 % decode.  The decode the pool buys is exactly the
2.3 GiB of arena it frees (12 084 -> 9 742 MiB): the KV of this model is ~13 KiB per token, i.e. ~2.6 GiB at
`-c 204800`, which a 24 GiB card can simply hold.  Goal A's **window** is the placement that buys the arena
back with no kernel change, and it is free up to its own depth -- past 64K its spilled tail costs 34 % prefill
(530.1 against 808.2 at 113K), so it is offered in the `runme` header, not made the default.

### What did NOT move the number (measured, so nobody re-derives it)

* `--threads` 7 / 12 / 14 / 16: 769 / 771 / 769 / 769 t/s at pp8K.  The CPU is not the critical path at this
  shape -- do not "fix" prefill with a thread count.
* `-ub 4096` against 2048: 771 against 769.  `--cache-ram 6144 -> 0`: 768.
* `GGML_CUDA_SLAB_HEADROOM_MIB 1536 -> 768`: 776 (+1 %) for +843 MiB of arena; not worth the OOM margin it
  exists to buy.
* `MOE_EXPERT_CACHE_MIB=4096`: 789 pp / 38 tg; `=0`: 890 pp / 10.8 tg.  The expert arena is a DECODE feature:
  with it off the prompt is the best measured (890) and decode collapses.  4 GiB is the prompt-leaning
  compromise if a batch-only workload ever needs one.

### Text

Same prompt, temp 0, 128 output tokens: `LLAMA_KV_REGION_POOL=16384`, the plain device KV and
`LLAMA_KV_RESIDENT_CELLS=65536` all produced `00f5b00b804d2ebf`.  On a 32K prompt at 48 output tokens the pool
arm produced a different prefix from the plain arm (one near-tie flip, stable across two prompts), which is
what a placement change is allowed to do and what `scripts/gate-kv-residency.sh` is the arbiter for.  The
shipped default is the plain device KV, i.e. block 15 unset -- the arm `patches/README.md` calls byte-identical
to a build without the block.

### The `runme` change (outside this repo, at the maintainer's request)

`LLAMA_KV_REGION_POOL` 16384 -> **0** (kill switch kept: `LLAMA_KV_REGION_POOL=16384 ./runme` tests the pool);
`--limit-host-experts 54` -> `${LME:-auto}`; `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` falls back on `/opt/rocm-10.1.0-gfx1100/lib`
before the gfx120X tree; the header rewritten around the table above.  Not a delivery change: the `runme` is
a host-side consumer config, and the shipped number that moved is the file's own.

**Conclusion for block 15 Goal B:** it is a *fittability* feature for a KV that does not fit, and a decode
feature on one that does.  It is not a prompt-path optimisation, and it must not be enabled on a prompt-heavy
server -- `ENVIRONMENT.md` 17.1 now says so.
