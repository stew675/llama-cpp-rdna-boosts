# A ferry timetable puzzle

Read the whole problem before you start. Work through it step by step, and state your reasoning
explicitly at every stage. Only give the final timetable at the very end.

A river has three landing stages in a line: North, Middle and South. A single ferry shuttles between
them seven times a day, and each of the seven crossings starts at one stage and ends at an adjacent
one, so a crossing is always either North<->Middle or Middle<->South. Exactly one crossing is made in
each of seven consecutive departure slots, numbered 1 to 7 from first to last. The following hold:

1. The ferry starts slot 1 at the North stage and finishes slot 7 at the South stage.
2. It never visits the same stage on two consecutive slots.
3. It uses the North<->Middle route exactly three times.
4. The first crossing of the day is not Middle<->South.
5. The ferry is at the Middle stage immediately after slot 3.
6. The South stage is visited at least twice as a departure point.
7. No two Middle<->South crossings are consecutive.

Work out every sequence of seven crossings that satisfies all seven conditions. For each valid
sequence, list the starting and ending stage of every slot. Then answer:

- How many distinct valid sequences are there?
- Is the Middle stage visited on slot 5 in every valid sequence, in none, or in some? Explain.
- If condition 7 is relaxed, how many additional sequences become valid?
- Suppose a second ferry is added that may make one additional crossing in the opposite direction in
  any slot, provided no stage has two ferries departing at once. Re-solve from scratch and say whether
  the previous count doubles, more than doubles, or less.
