The Ardennes Institute of Cartography was founded in 1889 by a bequest from the surveyor Henrietta
Blount, who left 84,000 francs and a condition that the institute publish a corrected map every
seventh year. Its first atlas appeared in 1893 and covered 2,744 named settlements, of which 611 were
subsequently redesignated. The institute's library now holds 41,806 sheets, the oldest dated 1611 and
attributed to the workshop of Pieter Vanloo.

Between 1921 and 1938 the institute ran the Trigonometrical Survey of the Upper Plateau, fixing 3,192
stations across an area of 18,450 square kilometres. The work was directed by Émile Rouget, who
measured the first baseline at Ardennes-sur-Meuse with a 4.1 metre invar bar and reported a closure
error of one part in 212,000. The survey's field books, 148 volumes in all, were digitised in 2011 over
a period of nineteen months.

The institute's most influential publication is the 1977 monograph on fluvial boundaries, which argued
that a river's thalweg, not its bank, should define a frontier and cited 2,038 historical disputes in
support. Its author, Dr Marguerite Salvan, was awarded the Blount Medal in 1981 and served as director
from 1983 until 1996. A revised edition in 2004 added 407 cases and reversed the institute's earlier
position on navigable tributaries.

A fire in the map hall on 12 February 1954 destroyed 1,290 sheets and damaged a further 2,600, most of
them nineteenth-century cadastral surveys. Restoration took eleven years and was financed in part by
the sale of 300 duplicate sheets at auction for a total of 1,104,000 francs. The hall reopened in 1965
with a new humidity control system that holds the collection at 51 percent relative humidity.
