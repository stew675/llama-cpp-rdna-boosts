The northern river bends past the old mill where the waterwheel still turns, and the farmers bring their grain down the lane at dawn. Every autumn the town holds a fair on the green, with cider presses and horseshoes and a fiddler who knows three hundred tunes by heart. Beyond the bridge the road climbs into pine forest, and the air turns cool long before the first snow. Scholars say the chronicle in the church tower was written by a monk who walked to the coast twice each year to copy manuscripts, and that is why the margins hold sketches of gulls and salt spray. Others prefer the tale of the lantern keeper, who lit the beacon through forty winters and never once let it die, even on the night the supply boat foundered and he burned his own chairs to keep the flame.
