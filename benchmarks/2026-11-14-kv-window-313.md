# 2026-11-14 -- issue #313: the windowed KV on a tensor split, the state-copy cliff, and what a spill actually costs

Issue: [`#313`](https://github.com/stew675/llama-cpp-rdna-boosts/issues/313) (reported by @mjohanning99, with
@kapitalmouse adding a second box).  Release: **r286**.  Block amended: **15** (the streamed KV).  Fix commit:
this release's block-15 amendment.

Reporter box: RX 9070 (gfx1201, PCIe 4.0 x16) + RX 7900 GRE (gfx1100, PCIe 3.0 **x4 through the chipset**),
repo r279, Qwen3.8-27B Q4_K_M, `-ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -fa on -np 1 -ub 1024`, `-c 65536 --kv-window 32768`.

## 1. The abort: a 2-D device copy on a region-backed VA

```
ROCm error: invalid argument
  current device: 1, in function ggml_backend_cuda_buffer_get_tensor_2d at ggml-cuda.cu:2445
  hipMemcpy2DAsync(pin, size, (const char *) tensor->data + offset + e0 * stride_tensor, ...)
#5  ggml_backend_cuda_buffer_get_tensor_2d
#6  ggml_backend_cuda_kv_region_get_tensor_2d      <- the BUFFER interface, i.e. the SYNC direction
#7  ggml_backend_tensor_get_2d
#8  ggml_backend_meta_get_tensor_impl
#9  ggml_backend_tensor_get_n
#11 llama_io_write_host::~llama_io_write_host      <- the batched path was NOT taken
#13 llama_context::state_seq_get_data
#14 server_slot::prompt_save
```

The stack names the **synchronous** buffer interface.  r204 (c)/(d) had translated the region's 2-D **async**
directions into 1-D copies; the two sync directions still issued `hipMemcpy2DAsync` against the windowed KV's
VMM region, whose backing is host-location mappings mixed into one reservation.  Why the batched path was not
taken: its cap is `LLAMA_STATE_ASYNC_MAX_MIB` = 256 MiB, and a 27B's 10 800-token slot state is ~670 MiB
(measured here: 323.93 MiB at 5 246 tokens, so `llama_io_write_host::flush_async` declines and the destructor
falls to `ggml_backend_tensor_get_n`).  The reporter's `LLAMA_STATE_ASYNC=0` arm gave the identical stack, as it
must.

Reproduced on this box **by construction, not by the driver**: `LLAMA_STATE_ASYNC_MAX_MIB=1` forces the sync
path on a 2-device `-sm tensor` server with `--kv-window`, and `LLAMA_KV_REGION_DEBUG=1` shows the region's
2-D read being taken (`… : 2-D read of 5246 rows x 544 B as 1-D copies (region-backed, no 2-D copy)`).
This driver (ROCm 10.1.0 and 7.14.1, gfx1201) *accepts* a tight 2-D D2H from a region VA, which is why r204's
own arms never failed here; the refusal is real on the reporter's stack (mixed gfx1100 + gfx1201, two boxes,
two KV types, `GGML_CUDA_BOUNCE_MIN=0` and default).  The delivery must not depend on a driver behaviour it
cannot validate, and `INVARIANTS.md` already records the same class twice (the movable-boundary slab, r183 and
r253: *"a cudaMemcpy2DAsync into a slab range is refused by the driver"*, and r204's mixed-backing note).

**Fix (block 15, `ggml-cuda.cu`).**  A region-backed buffer's 2-D directions are **1-D only, in both
directions and in both interfaces**.  The block is gathered/scattered on the device with
`ggml_cuda_cpy_2d_rows()` (new, `cpy.cu`: vectorised 16-byte units, row from the grid — the `cpy_2d_vec16`
pattern the driver-refusal guard already uses) through a grow-only per-device staging buffer, and crosses the
link as ONE contiguous copy per chunk.  A pageable host end is received/packed through the reused pinned
chunk.  `ggml_backend_cuda_get/set_tensor_2d_async` now call the same helpers through the buffer interface
(they used to loop one *synchronous* copy per row).

## 2. The cliff that came with it: 4 686 ms -> 24.5 ms per slot save+load

`scripts/gate-state-pipe.sh` (2 x R9700, `-sm tensor`, 35B-A3B Q4_K_M, `-ncmoe 10`, `-c 16384`,
`KV_WINDOW=4096`, 3 cycles).  "prompt cache update took", median of 7:

| arm | before (r285 + window) | after | ratio to `sync` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `pipe`   | **4 686.16 ms** | **24.49 ms** | 0.61x |
| `copy0`  | 4 713.67 ms | 27.24 ms | 0.67x |
| `pipe1`  | 4 695.78 ms | 26.14 ms | 0.66x |
| `read1`  | 518.00 ms | 33.65 ms | 0.80x |
| `sync`   | 41.00 ms | 42.21 ms | 1.00x |

191x on the pipelined arm, and the batched path is back to being *faster* than the synchronous one, which is
its whole reason to exist.  The cause is the same per-row loop: a split splice's row is 544 B (q8_0, 4 KV
heads, 2 devices), so one save is 128 tensors x 5 246 synchronous copies.  A 27B at 10 800 tokens (670 MiB of
state) is 2x that again, which is what a `--cache-ram` server pays on **every** slot handoff.

`gate-state-pipe.sh` gained **HARD 4** for this: the batched save+load must be within `SAVE_SLOW_X` (3x) of the
synchronous arm's.  The cliff it guards is 114x, so the bound has 38x of margin; both of this release's runs
read 0.5-0.8x.  Text is byte-identical on every arm in both runs (with and without a window).

The no-window arm is unchanged (`pipe` 19.4 -> 21.1 ms, `sync` 40.2 -> 41.4 ms, board noise): the region's
helpers are only reached by a region-backed buffer.

## 3. What a spill actually costs per decode token

Qwen3.8-27B Q4_K_XL, `-ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -fa on -b 2048 -ub 1024 -t 8`, `prompts/prose-rdna-boosts.txt`
(5 246 tokens, twice = 10 492, six times = 31 000), 32 greedy tokens, `-c 65536`, one R9700, **nothing else
running**.

| request | window | prompt t/s | decode t/s | spill |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 5 246 | off | 1277.1 | **30.5** | nothing |
| 5 246 | `4096` / `2048` | 1267 / 1266 | 30.5 | nothing (under one 4 MiB map unit) |
| 5 246 | `512` / `1` | 1259 / 1257 | **27.3** (-10 %) | one unit per tensor |
| 10 492 | off | 1264.1 | **30.0** | nothing |
| 10 492 | `4096` | 1261.6 | **27.0** (-10 %) | one unit |
| 10 492 | `2048` / `1` | 1262.6 / 1254.2 | **24.3** (-19 %) | two units |
| 31 000 | off | 1156.3 | **29.0** | nothing |
| 31 000 | `8192` | 1135.4 | **16.8** (-42 %) | six units |
| 31 000 | `4096` | 1130.9 | **15.6** (-46 %) | seven units |

**Two devices, `-sm tensor` (0,2), 10 492 tokens:** off 1979.0 / **45.3**; `8192` 1930.1 / **38.3** (-15 %);
`4096` 1920.3 / 38.5 (-15 %).  The two cards read their own slices in parallel, so a split costs what one card
costs.

Read the columns together: **decode is linear in the number of spilled 4 MiB map units per tensor** (one unit
= -10 %, two = -19 %, six = -42 %), and the prompt is nearly untouched (-1 to -2 %).  One extra unit per tensor
costs 4.6 ms of a 60 ms step at 31 000 tokens, i.e. **15-30 GB/s of attended bytes** -- close to the link, not
below it.

**The read rate, measured directly.**  A linear 16-byte-vector pass over a mapped pinned host buffer measures
**27.9 GB/s** (`hipHostMalloc` + `hipHostGetDevicePointer`) and **29.0 GB/s** (`cuMemCreate` with
`CU_MEM_LOCATION_TYPE_HOST`, i.e. exactly the mapping a spill uses) on gfx1201 over PCIe 4.0 x16 -- the same
class of number as the copy engine's ~29-55 GB/s.  The device's in-place read rate is now calibrated at startup
(`ggml_backend_cuda_kv_region_spill_read_gbps`, one cold 128-256 MiB linear pass) and both the window's log line
and `--fit`'s per-token estimate divide by it:

```
in-place host read (a spilled KV cell) measures 16.06 GB/s over 128.0 MiB
WINDOWED KV ENABLED: … every spilled cell is read IN PLACE over the PCIe link on every step (16.0 GB/s in
place, calibrated, the measured in-place read rate), so a token at the full context reads 2040.0 MiB in about
124.1 ms -- and that is linear in how far a request outgrows the window, so judge W against the depth you
actually run; a card that reads its host aperture below its DMA link scales it up from there.
```

**Two measurement traps this section first fell into** (both recorded because they cost this session a wrong
conclusion, and a reader with the same numbers in hand would draw the same wrong one):

* **`LLAMA_KV_REGION_DEBUG=1` disables the measurement.**  The bench harness exported it to get the mapping
  census, and the knob verifies the whole region on every grow (its own doc: *"10-40x slower on a spilling arm --
  never quote a timing from it"*).  Every spilling arm then read 4.8 / 2.6 t/s, i.e. "catastrophic", while the
  same arms measured 27.0 / 24.3 t/s with the knob off.  The *non*-spilling arms were fine, which is what made
  the contaminated curve look plausible.
* **A strided probe is not the shape of a spill.**  The first calibration read 544 B every 34 816 B (the shape
  of a *per-head* gather) and reported 0.07-1.1 GB/s.  A spilled tensor's leading cells are one contiguous run,
  so the probe measured a pattern no step performs; it is linear now.

**Why the reporters saw what they saw.**  Their spill is the same physics with a much slower read path and a
much deeper request.  @mjohanning99: 62 564 tokens with `--kv-window 32768` on a 27B f16 KV is ~30k cells past
the window, i.e. a multi-GB spill per step; on the 9070 alone (PCIe 4.0 x16) that read 10.6 t/s (consistent with
its 28 GB/s link), and on the 7900 GRE (PCIe 3.0 **x4 through the chipset**, 3.25 GB/s of DMA) 0.24 t/s, i.e.
under 1 GB/s of attended spill -- a 3x gap to its own DMA rate, which is the number `--fit` now measures per
device instead of assuming.  @kapitalmouse's `KV_WINDOW=1` arm (18.5 vs 46.2 t/s) is the same shape at a
shallower depth.  Nothing about the mechanism is wrong for a fittability feature -- the KV does not fit and the
alternative is an OOM -- but the **cost is real and it is the depth past the window times the read rate**, and
a window at or above your request depth never pays anything (rows 2-3 above).

## 3b. Would a streamed ring (resident head + streamed middle + resident tail) help?

Asked while reviewing this record, and worth answering with the numbers because the intuition is sound and the
answer is hardware-dependent.  The proposal: keep `W/2` cells resident at the head of the cache, stream the
middle from host through a ring buffer, keep `W/2` at the tail; the head's compute masks the middle's transfer,
and the tail is the fast path we already have.

**It cannot help on this box, because there is no transfer to mask.**  Attention reads *every* cell of a sequence
on every decode step, so what a step pays is `bytes in host / read rate`, independent of the layout: head, middle
and tail are read the same number of times.  The measured numbers say the in-place read is already at link speed:

| arm | host-mapped KV | step time vs resident | implied rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 31 000 tokens, `W=8192` | **768 MiB** (boundary 24 MiB/tensor, 32 tensors) | 59.5 ms vs 34.5 ms (+25 ms) | **30.7 GB/s** |
| 5 246 tokens, `W=1` | 128 MiB | 36.6 ms vs 32.8 ms (+3.8 ms) | **33.7 GB/s** |

against a copy-engine link of ~29 GB/s on the same device.  A ring would move the same bytes with the same engine
class at the same rate, add a VRAM ring, and add a remap + copy per tensor per step: strictly worse here.

**It can help where the aperture read is slower than the DMA path, and there the right shape is per-layer, not
head/tail.**  The #313 reporter's gfx1100 behind a PCIe 3.0 x4 chipset reads its spill at under 1 GB/s against
3.25 GB/s of DMA -- a ~3x gap a ring would recover.  But the win comes from *using the copy engine*, not from the
head/tail split: the ring only needs to hold the spill of the tensors in flight (the region holds 32 tensors of
24 MiB at the arm above, so 2-3 tensors = ~50-75 MiB, independent of how deep the spill is), with tensor `t+1`'s
fill overlapping layer `t`'s compute.  That is a per-layer streamed ring, and on a bandwidth-bound step it is
`bytes / DMA_rate` instead of `bytes / aperture_rate`.  It needs the hardware that shows the gap to validate
(we do not have a gfx1100 here), and it is recorded here as the candidate for one, not as a delivery change.

## 4. Found along the way (implemented, not filed)

* **`--kv-window` / `--kv-attendance-pool` were example-restricted** (`SERVER`, `PERPLEXITY`, `CLI`), so they
  were absent from `llama-batched-bench`, `llama-bench`, `llama-parallel`, `llama-speculative` and every other
  common-parser tool -- silently, including their `LLAMA_ARG_*` env forms, because `common_params_parser_init`
  does not even *register* an option outside its examples.  They are common options now, like `-lme`/`-lm`.
* **`llama-bench` has its own argument parser** and knew nothing about the window (`--kv-window` was rejected,
  and `LLAMA_ARG_KV_WINDOW` was applied by the common parser it does not use) -- @kapitalmouse's report.  It
  takes `--kv-window <auto|N>` now (and prints it as its own column when it is not the default).
* **`LLAMA_KV_REGION_DEBUG=1` costs ~15x decode on a split** (38.1 -> 2.7 t/s measured), because it verifies the
  whole region on every grow.  Already documented as "never quote a timing from it"; the numbers above were all
  taken with it off.
